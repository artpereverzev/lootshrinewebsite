v1.6.3 LATEST CONTENT + FIXES

THE BOARD FILLS UP — 40 QUESTS, 20 DIVINE ITEMS, 6 EVENTS, AND SOME BROKEN PROMISES KEPT

Hey, it’s Tim. This one is mostly about things that ask you a question. Forty new quests, twenty new divine items, six new events. And running underneath all of it, a smaller and slightly more embarrassing theme — several things in this game were promising you something they didn’t deliver. Backstab advertised a guaranteed dodge and gave you a coin flip. Merchant skills said “this run” and lasted forever. Those are fixed too. — Tim (solo developer)

THE QUEST BOARD IS A PROPER SYSTEM NOW

  • The board was the weakest screen in the camp. Three quests, and once you cleared them the tab was dead for the rest of the run. Worse, rewards were locked in the moment you accepted — so taking a quest early, when your Magic Find and gold multipliers were low, permanently gave you a worse payout than taking the same quest later.
  • Forty new quests, spread across daily and rare. The board now refills as you clear it instead of draining toward empty, so there’s always something live on it.
  • Six new kinds of objective, covering the loops the board never touched: selling back to the shop, picking up loot, earning gold, gearing up, elite fights, and using your class skill. Combined with the camp objectives — sacrificing, merging, forging, donating, claiming blessings, activating set bonuses — nearly everything you actually do now counts toward something.
  • Legendary quests climb their own ladder. Clear one and a harder version takes its place, with a bigger payout. Clear that, and it steps up again. The legendary tab doesn’t run dry; it just gets steeper.
  • Two details worth knowing:
    • Rewards are calculated when you claim, not when you accept. Take a quest on floor 5 and cash it in on floor 40 and you’re paid at floor-40 rates. Accepting early is no longer a quiet mistake.
    • It looks the part now. Parchment notices pinned to wood, instead of a list of rows. Small thing. It reads like a board you’d actually walk up to.

TWENTY NEW DIVINE ITEMS — THE COLLECTION DOUBLES

  • Divine items went from 21 to 41. They still drop the same way: from bosses and divine chests, rare and run-defining.
  • What changed is the design brief. Each new one carries its own rule rather than a larger number. A few of them:
    • The Unwriting Edge — “It does not cut armour. It cuts the sentence that said there was armour.”
    • Verdict Everlasting — “The Judge does not strike. It simply continues to be right, loudly.”
    • The Hungering Coil — “It does not want the world. It wants the next thing, forever.”
    • Wyrmscale Grips — “Three scales thick. The fourth blow is the one that counts.”
    • Crown of the Repeated Hour — “You have already worn this. You will wear it again in a moment.”
    • Warden’s Last Refusal — “The Warden’s post has one rule: not through here. Not yet.”
  • I’d rather you found out what they do by finding them, so that’s all you get here.

SIX NEW EVENTS

  • The descent gets six new encounters, and most of them are trades rather than gifts.
  • The Vaultkeeper — a ledger-keeper in grey, pen already uncapped. “Everyone needs something sooner than they can afford it.” He lends now. He collects later.
  • The Mask Merchant — three masks on a pole, facing outward. “They are not jewellery. Whichever you take, you take to the end.” She deals in scars you keep.
  • The Mirror That Lies — a sheet of silvered glass, taller than you, and your reflection is already holding its weapon differently to yours. You fight yourself.
  • The Stray — something large lying against a wall, breathing badly. It’s been in a fight and it didn’t win. It watches you approach without getting up.
  • The Two Doors — the corridor forks. One door is scratched over with tally marks; the other has handprints on it, and they aren’t all the same size. Your choice changes the battle ahead before you walk into it.
  • The Hollow Choir — five robed shapes, hoods empty, holding a note that was already here. They’ll trade two unspent skill points for a glyph from the Author’s Word, up to three times a run. And they always hand one over — even if you already own every glyph, because a duplicate is still worth salvaging. No dead trades.

THE STRAY, AND THE PACK THAT HAD NO DOOR

  • Adopting the Stray costs you nothing to keep. It fights alongside your chosen companions rather than pushing one out of the pack — it’s a bonus ally, not a trade.
  • The pack picker is findable now. It existed. You could not reasonably discover it.
  • A newly unlocked beast fills a free slot on its own, instead of unlocking into nothing and waiting for you to notice.

YOU CAN PROTECT AN ITEM NOW

  • This one came straight from bug reports, and every one of them was the same story: I had the piece I was building around, I hit Sacrifice All, and it was gone.
  • Star an item and every bulk operation skips it. Sell All, Sacrifice All, sell by rarity, sacrifice by rarity — all of them step over a favourited piece. The one thing you were saving cannot be swept away by a mistimed tap.
  • The star shows up where you need to see it, too: the altar, the forge and the empower cells all display it, alongside the upgrade arrow.

TWO NEW LEGENDARY MERCHANT ARTIFACTS

  • The Golden Horseshoe — “Nailed above every honest trader’s door. The cutpurses of the lower floors have learned to walk the other way.” Nothing can be taken from you. Not from your bag, not off your back. It also pays 10% more hero XP and gold.
  • Message in a Bottle — “The letter inside is never the same twice, and it is never signed. Whoever is sending these knows exactly how deep you have gone.” A parcel waits for you on every new floor, and your Magic Find decides what’s inside.

TWO PROMISES, NOW KEPT

  • The Rogue’s Backstab. It advertised a guaranteed dodge and delivered a coin flip. That’s not a tuning problem, that’s the description being wrong. Now it arms Vanish — a real buff, visible in the strip — and the next enemy attack misses outright. It waits until it’s spent. No timer, no roll.
  • Merchant skills. They now appear as buff pips in battle, with their own art, so you can actually see what you bought. And they last one run rather than forever — which is what the description always said they did.

WORD FRAGMENTS CAN NO LONGER BE LOST

  • A few of you reported glyphs disappearing between sessions. You were right, and there wasn’t one cause — there were three:
    • a rewrite racing cloud sync, where whichever finished last won
    • a merge that could bring already-spent copies back and then lose the live ones
    • a save arriving from the cloud emptier than the one already on your device, and being trusted anyway
  • All three are closed. The device’s copy is never overwritten by a thinner one, spent fragments stay spent, and rewrites can’t collide with a sync any more.
  • And because this is exactly the kind of bug that comes back the next time someone adds a field: there’s now an audit that fails loudly if new save data is ever added without merge rules attached. It’s the fix for the class of bug, not just these three.

SMALLER THINGS

  • Eighteen new prestige avatars, for achievements that never had one.
  • A launch screen with proper credit — the people who helped build this get their names on it.
  • A force-refresh button on the leaderboards, for when you know your score landed and the board hasn’t caught up.
  • Surpassing a fallen run pays souls that scale with how deep that run went. Beating a ghost from floor 80 is worth more than beating one from floor 12, which it always should have been.

FIXES

  • A battle could stall when an enemy died to a lingering effect. If the killing blow came from something already on the field — a burn tick, a delayed effect — rather than a direct hit, the turn could fail to hand back to you. Resolved.
  • Stun wore the wrong icon. It was showing frost. It’s lightning now.
  • Enemy status badges overflowed their row when an enemy carried enough effects at once. They fit now.
  • Layout on iPhone SE and 17e. A batch of small-screen problems: cut-off borders, a quest banner sitting off-screen, and a pause button overlapping the EXP bar. All fixed on both.

LOCALIZATION

  • Everything above — forty quest descriptions, twenty divine items with their lore, six events with every branch and outcome, two artifacts, and every new label and alert — ships day one in all 10 supported languages: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, and Simplified Chinese.

THE DISCORD

  • The server’s still the fastest way to reach me — patch talk, build chat, and a bug-reports channel I genuinely read. Several fixes in this update came from there, including the word-fragment loss and the Sacrifice All complaint that became the favourite star: discord.gg/McptmPeddx

v1.6.2 SHRINE REWORK

THE STONE WAKES — PRAY STONE & DONATION ALTAR REBUILT AROUND REAL DECISIONS

Hey, it’s Tim. The whole game runs on one idea: every item is a decision — equip it, absorb it, or sacrifice it forever. This update is about the one place that idea didn’t apply: the shrine. The pray stone handed out blessings you stacked without thinking, and the donation altar took a capped trickle of gold you dumped without noticing. Neither asked you anything. Both got rebuilt. — Tim (solo developer)

THE PRAY STONE SLEEPS — AND WAKES

  • Praying used to be a button you pressed a few times every floor. Each blessing stacked on the last, so a run quietly accumulated a pile of buffs nobody could remember choosing.
  • Now the stone sleeps. It wakes after floor 1, then every tenth floor — 20, 30, 40 — all the way to 110. Each waking offers you two blessings drawn from a pool of twenty-four, art and all. Tap one to read exactly what it does before you commit. Take one.
  • The rule that makes it interesting: only one blessing is active at a time. Choose a new one and the old one goes. So every waking is a real question — keep building around what you have, or trade it for what’s on offer? Blessings you take don’t return that run, which means the pool thins as you climb and the late wakings ask harder questions than the early ones.
  • Three details worth knowing:
    • The offer waits for you. Leave the camp without choosing and the two blessings stay on the stone — on the next floor, and the one after — until the stone’s next waking rolls a fresh pair. Forgetting doesn’t cost you the gift.
    • Walking away isn’t free. Your current blessing burns out when the stone wakes again, whether you choose a new one or not. Declining used to be the quietly optimal move; now the choice is real in both directions.
    • You can see what’s guiding you. Your active blessing shows as its own icon in the battle buff strip — tap it for the full detail. Blessings with an armed state (Second Dawn’s revive, Stonewill’s first-hit shield) show separately while they’re primed.

DONATIONS — THE CAP IS GONE, THE ROAD IS LONG

  • The old altar accepted a couple of donations per floor and paid out a random stat bump. Telemetry was blunt about it: almost nobody used it, and I don’t blame you.
  • Now every coin counts, with no cap — the altar always takes your gold. And instead of random crumbs, donations feed one long road with eight named milestones, from 1,000 gold all the way to 1,000,000. Each milestone pays permanent rewards for the rest of the run: extra sacrifice and absorb capacity, flat Attack, Defense and HP, crit. Tap any milestone on the track to see exactly what it pays before you save toward it.
  • The little things that make it feel right:
    • A slider, not buttons. Donate any amount from 100 gold up to everything you’re carrying — one drag, then MAX when you’re feeling devout. The number on screen is exactly the number spent.
    • Devotion makes coins heavier. Shrine devotion perks and the Shrine Favor upgrade in the Soul Forge make each donated coin count for more on the track — late-game builds can genuinely chase the 1M stone.
    • Your progress is visible in battle. Milestone rewards that stay armed — the Kindled Altar’s bonus sacrifices, the shop’s Rare guarantee — show as pips in the buff strip, same as everything else that’s live in a fight.
  • Between the stone and the altar, the camp is now a place where you make decisions, not a place where you press buttons on the way to the next floor.

MORE OPTIMIZATION

  • 1.6.1 was the big performance pass; this update keeps going. The camp, the menus and the skill tree now sit completely still when you do — zero background work on idle screens — and each hit in combat costs less to process, which adds up fast in splash-damage fights. Cooler device, longer battery, especially on long sessions and older hardware.
  • One follow-up on a 1.6.1 feature: the mid-battle pop-up that offered Reduced Effects when your device ran hot is gone. It meant well, but it interrupted fights at exactly the wrong moment. The Reduced Effects switch is still in Settings, one tap away, whenever you want it — the game just won’t tap you on the shoulder about it mid-swing.

FIXES

  • Splitting elites could end the battle early. If the last enemy of a floor’s last battle had the split-on-death modifier and died quickly, the battle could count itself as won while the split was still happening — sending you to the shrine past two newly-spawned halves. The victory check now waits for the whole death to resolve, spawns included.
  • The Continue alert was living in the past. Leaving the shrine could warn you about “remaining prayers” — a thing that no longer exists. It now tells you what’s actually true: whether a waking is still unclaimed at the stone.
  • Translation pass. A batch of strings that showed in English regardless of your language — including several shrine and battle labels — now translate properly. Found four, audited the rest of the game, fixed thirty-plus.

LOCALIZATION

  • Everything above — twenty-four blessing names and descriptions, eight milestone cards, every new label and alert — ships day one in all 10 supported languages: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, and Simplified Chinese.

THE DISCORD IS OPEN

  • This build adds a Discord button on the main menu — the official LootShrine server is live. Patch notes, dev updates, build talk, and a bug-reports channel I actually read. If you’ve ever wanted to tell me which blessing is overpowered, that’s the place: discord.gg/McptmPeddx

v1.6.1 PERFORMANCE & FIXES

COOLER, SMOOTHER, SHARPER — WHOLE-GAME PERFORMANCE PASS & NEW SHATTER DEATH VFX

Hey, it’s Tim. This one is almost entirely under the hood — and it’s the update I’m happiest about in a while. LootShrine got a top-to-bottom performance pass: battles run smoother, your phone runs cooler, and the battery lasts noticeably longer on a long session. Along the way defeated enemies got a new death effect, and four bugs got squashed — including one that could genuinely stall the tutorial. — Tim (solo developer)

THE BIG ONE — THE WHOLE GAME IS LIGHTER

  • If you’ve ever finished a long session and noticed your phone was warm, that was real, and it was my fault. A game screen only needs to redraw when something changes — but LootShrine was redrawing constantly, even when you were standing still in the shrine reading your stats. A few drifting gold motes were enough to keep the whole screen re-rendering every frame, forever. Multiply that across the menu, shrine, skill tree, leaderboards, customization, and battle, and the game was quietly doing a lot of work to show you a still image.
  • That’s fixed everywhere. All the ambient motion — drifting motes, glowing pulses, breathing icons, shimmering frames, the camp fire — now runs on the system’s own animation engine instead of being redrawn by the game each frame. The animations all look exactly the same. Nothing was removed or slowed down; the game just stopped doing the work by hand.
  • What you should notice:
    • Idle screens are genuinely idle. Sitting in the shrine, menu or skill tree now costs a fraction of what it did.
    • Battles are smoother, especially the messy ones — six enemies on screen with splash damage flying was the worst case, and it’s the case that improved most.
    • Less heat, longer battery. The difference is biggest on long runs, which is exactly when it mattered least before.
  • For the curious: on my test device, an idle screen went from a state where the display was being fully re-composited sixty times a second to one where the game does essentially nothing between your taps. Heavy combat moments that used to peg well past a full CPU core now sit comfortably under half of one. Older devices should feel this the most.

NEW — ENEMIES SHATTER INTO PIXELS

  • Defeated enemies used to simply fade out under the death poof. Now they break apart — the sprite shatters into a burst of pixel chunks that fly outward, tumble, and fall, timed to the exact moment the death effect lands.
  • It’s a small thing, but kills read much better now, particularly when you wipe several enemies at once: each one bursts in turn instead of the whole row quietly disappearing.

FIXES

  • Loot flying to your bag. Occasionally an item — usually one out of a multi-drop — would snap instantly into your bag instead of flying there. The animation was being swallowed before it started. Every drop now makes the full arc.
  • Enemies vanishing early. When one attack killed several enemies, all the corpses disappeared at once while the death effects were still playing out one by one, so later bursts went off over empty ground. Each enemy now stays on the field until its own death effect fires.
  • Tutorial could stall. If you hit hard enough to destroy the training dummy in a single blow, the tutorial could get stuck waiting for a defeat that had already happened — leaving you with no target and a greyed-out attack button. Fixed on both ends; a one-shot kill now advances the tutorial correctly.
  • Companions missing after the tutorial. If you had summon Fragments socketed in The Word, your companions weren’t appearing in the run that continues out of the tutorial. They now show up from the first Floor 1 battle, as intended. (Beastmasters were losing their starting pet the same way.)

UPGRADE BADGES NOW ON BY DEFAULT

  • The little arrow that marks a bag or shop item as better than what you’re wearing has been in Settings since 1.6.0 — but tucked behind a toggle, which meant most people never knew it existed. It’s now on by default for anyone who hasn’t already made a choice about it.
  • If you’d rather read your own gear, it’s one tap to turn off, and the sensitivity slider is still there — dial it down and only significant upgrades get flagged. If you turned badges off before this update, they stay off.

LOCALIZATION

  • No new text this time — the whole update is performance, effects and fixes, so every language is already up to date. All 10 supported languages carry the changes: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, and Simplified Chinese.

NOTHING ABOUT THE GAME CHANGED — SAME TOWER, JUST LIGHTER TO CARRY. CLIMB WELL, AND STAY COOL. — TIM

v1.6.0 MAJOR UPDATE

THE AUTHOR’S WORD — FOUR NEW HEROES, TWENTY NEW FLOORS, AND A TOWER THAT NO LONGER ENDS

Hey, it’s Tim. This is the biggest update LootShrine has ever had — by a wide margin. Four new heroes take the roster to fourteen. Twenty new floors open past the Origin, where the tower stops behaving itself. And a whole new meta layer, The Author’s Word, lets you compose permanent powers out of Fragments you collect up there. Everything below is included in the base game — no new purchase, no season pass, nothing gated behind a paywall. — Tim (solo developer)

NEW — FOUR HEROES (14 TOTAL)

  • Sylva, The Huntress — the Ranger. “Every target tells you where it will die. You just have to listen.”
    • Passive DEADEYE — patience sharpens the shot. The longer you read a target, the more certainly you hit it.
    • MARK your quarry, then punish it. Marked enemies take more from every arrow you land.
    • Skill HUNTER’S VOLLEY — a rain of arrows across the field.
    • Exclusive TRACKER’S SIGIL slot, plus two hunter sets.
  • Vael, The Timekeeper — the Chronomancer. A hero who plays the turn order itself rather than the enemy.
    • Passive FORESIGHT — glimpse what’s coming before it lands.
    • Spend Time to bend a battle: skill QUICKEN buys you an extra action in a turn, and REWIND undoes a moment you’d rather hadn’t happened.
    • Exclusive FRACTURED HOURGLASS slot, plus two time-woven sets.
  • Kenzo, The Wandering Blade — the Ronin. “A hundred strikes waste the blade. I need only one.”
    • Passive COUNTERSTANCE — defending isn’t passive. Weather a blow and answer it with a riposte.
    • Bank Focus through the fight, then spend it on IAIDO — a single drawing cut whose damage scales with everything you saved.
    • Exclusive RONIN’S WHETSTONE slot, plus two blade sets.
  • Zari, The Tempest — the Stormcaller. “I do not fight a man. I fight the room he stands in.”
    • Passive CONDUCTOR — the storm builds on itself the longer it rages.
    • A four-spell toolkit you swap between as the fight demands: CHAIN LIGHTNING, STATIC SHROUD, THUNDERCLAP and EYE OF THE STORM.
    • Exclusive TEMPEST TOTEM slot, plus two storm sets.
  • Each hero ships with a full skill tree, stat-trait nodes, dedicated pixel-art VFX, Codex lore, per-class leaderboards and mastery achievements — the same depth every existing class has. Unlock them by climbing, same as the rest.

NEW — BEYOND THE ORIGIN (FLOORS 101–120)

  • The Primordial holds the Origin at Floor 100. Something older is waiting past it. Twenty new floors across two acts — and the climb continues straight through, no menu, no reset.
  • Beyond isn’t more of the same floors with bigger numbers. Every fight up there is built as a puzzle with a rule you have to read before you can win:
    • Enemies that reflect everything except skills.
    • Linked wards that must be brought down together or not at all.
    • Phase-shifting skins that refuse damage until the moment turns.
    • Living glyphs that rewrite the rules of the battle while they breathe.
  • Two new guardians: THE THRESHOLD WARDEN at 110, and at 120 — THE UNWRITTEN. Both come with escorts, so the final stretch is a fight against a formation, not a single healthbar.
  • Roughly thirty new named enemies with full Bestiary entries, new music for both acts, story beats on the way up, and an ending sequence for anyone who reaches the top.
  • Beyond is free. It’s part of the base game, not an expansion.

NEW — THE AUTHOR’S WORD

  • Past the Origin you start finding Word Fragments, and a new tab opens up where you compose them into your Word — a permanent meta layer that carries across every future run.
  • 37 Fragments in three kinds:
    • GLYPHS — raw statlines. Attack, Max HP, crit, defense, gold find, lifesteal, thorns.
    • VERBS — triggered rules that fire mid-combat. Silence the first turn, delete an enemy outright, cheat death once per run, auto-win a floor.
    • CLAUSES — abilities grafted from other heroes. Run the Monk’s combo on a Knight. Summon a companion as a Mage.
  • Sockets are limited and every Fragment costs Ink, so your Word is a real build decision, not a checklist. More sockets and a deeper inkwell are earned through the Soul Forge.
  • Duplicates aren’t dead loot. Merge a spare copy to raise a Fragment’s tier and strengthen its effect, or salvage it for Souls. Rarity runs from Common to Legendary, and rarer Fragments hit considerably harder.
  • New Soul Forge upgrades on a dedicated WORDS tab: more sockets of each kind, a deeper Ink budget, amplified Glyph statlines, and a fortune upgrade that biases drops toward rarer Fragments.
  • Fragments drop from Beyond floors and from a new cursed chest that can appear after Adventure bosses — so you can start building your Word before you ever reach 101.

NEW — COMPANIONS FOR EVERY CLASS

  • Battle pets are no longer Beastmaster-only. Every hero can now field a companion, and the old placeholder strike has been retired in favour of a real one.
  • Three summon Clauses bring their own companions to the field — a Warden that shields, a Cinder that burns, and a Reaper that hunts. Socket one and it fights beside you regardless of class.
  • Companions use the same HP bars, revive rules and scaling as the Beastmaster pack, and each has a Codex entry that unlocks when you first meet it.

NEW — ACCURACY & EVASION

  • A new stat pair that makes the back row and fast enemies feel genuinely different to fight. Accuracy determines whether your blow lands; Evasion is the enemy’s chance to slip it.
  • Misses show a clear EVADE popup so a bad turn never feels like a bug.
  • Accuracy comes from your class baseline, item affixes, skill-tree nodes and a new Keen Eye upgrade in the Soul Forge — so if evasive enemies are giving you trouble, there’s a concrete answer.
  • The full formula, sources and caps are documented in the Codex’s stat card.

QUALITY OF LIFE

  • Sacrifice All and Sacrifice by Rarity at the Shrine — the same bulk controls the camp Armory already had for selling. No more tapping through thirty items one at a time.
  • The Word tab renders as an open book, with your Fragments laid out across the pages and active effects broken down so you can see exactly what your Word is doing for you.
  • Your Word’s contribution now shows in the hero detail SOURCES breakdown, alongside gear, sets and skill tree.

GAME CENTER & CODEX

  • A new WORD COLLECTOR leaderboard tracking every Fragment you’ve ever collected, from any source. Lifetime and cumulative, like Enemy Slayer.
  • Full per-class leaderboard suites for all four new heroes, plus mastery and deep-run achievements for each.
  • A new Words achievement category, and Beyond achievements for crossing the threshold and for finishing what waits at 120.
  • Codex additions throughout: the four new heroes, both Beyond guardians, thirty new enemies, the Word Fragments, and the new companions.

LOCALIZATION

  • Everything above ships fully translated in all 10 supported languages: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, and Simplified Chinese. Every new hero, Fragment, enemy, boss and Codex entry included.

STILL ONE PURCHASE

  • The first 10 floors are free. One $2.99 unlock still buys the entire game forever — all 14 heroes, all 120 floors, Beyond the Origin, The Author’s Word, and every update after this one.
  • No ads. No energy timers. No subscriptions. No loot boxes.
  • Supporter packs remain cosmetic only — avatars, frames and nameplates, zero gameplay power. Most cosmetics are still earned free by playing.

THE TOWER NEVER ENDED AT THE ORIGIN. GO FIND OUT WHAT’S BEEN WRITING IT. — TIM

v1.5.8 POLISH & FIX

SHOW OFF YOUR COLORS — NAMEPLATES NOW VISIBLE TO EVERYONE & LEADERBOARDS FIT SMALL SCREENS

Hey, it’s Tim. Small, focused update this time — but one of these fixes is something a lot of you asked about directly. Supporter nameplates finally do what they were always meant to do, and the leaderboards behave themselves on smaller screens. — Tim (solo developer)

YOUR NAMEPLATE IS NOW VISIBLE TO EVERYONE

  • When nameplates arrived, they had a quiet flaw: the animated background behind your name — the gradient-and-particle plate that supporters unlock — only showed up on your own device. You’d pick a gorgeous plate, admire it on your own row, and nobody else in the tower could actually see it. Which kind of defeats the point of showing off.
  • That’s fixed. Your nameplate now travels with you and displays to other players:
    • On the global and friends leaderboards, behind your row.
    • In the Hall of the Fallen — on your fallen-run cards, so your final stands look the part.
    • On friend-challenge cards, when you send a run someone’s way.
  • Your portrait and frame already crossed over to other players; now your nameplate rides along with them, so your whole look is consistent everywhere the tower sees you — not just in your own reflection.
  • A couple of practical notes, since a few of you will ask:
    • Nameplates are a supporter cosmetic — purely visual, no gameplay power. If you don’t have one set, nothing changes for you.
    • Like your avatar and frame, your nameplate propagates to other players the next time you post a score (finish a run). Play a run after updating and the rest of the tower will start seeing it.
    • Everyone sees the correct plates once they’re on this version — and portraits and frames are completely unaffected by the change.
  • For the curious: the reason this was broken is that the nameplate was being matched to you by an identifier that Game Center deliberately scrambles between players for privacy, so the lookup only ever found your own plate. The fix routes the nameplate through the same channel your avatar and frame already use — one that every device can read — so it Just Works now.

LEADERBOARDS FIT YOUR SCREEN AGAIN

  • With the roster now at ten classes, the class-filter row across the top of the leaderboards had quietly outgrown its space — on smaller iPhones the All + class buttons ran off the right edge, dragging the rest of the board with them and clipping names and scores.
  • The filter row is now horizontally scrollable: it takes exactly the width it’s given, so nothing spills off-screen. Swipe the row to reach any class, and the leaderboard underneath stays properly aligned.
  • On larger screens it looks the same as before — this only kicks in when space is tight.

LOCALIZATION

  • Both changes ship fully translated in all 10 supported languages: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, and Simplified Chinese.

v1.5.7 MAJOR UPDATE

ENTER THE MONK — TENTH CLASS, LIVE COSMETIC PREVIEWS & BIGGER RIVALRIES

Hey, it’s Tim. This is a big one. LootShrine 1.5.7 adds the tenth playable class, gives supporter cosmetics a way to finally show off to the rest of the world, and folds in a pile of quality-of-life and balance work I’ve wanted to ship for a while. Everything below is free for everyone unless it says otherwise. — Tim (solo developer)

NEW — THE MONK (10TH CLASS, FREE FOR EVERYONE)
